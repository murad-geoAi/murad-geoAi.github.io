About

I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of River-Coastal Science and Engineering at Tulane University in New Orleans, Louisiana, and a Graduate Research Assistant in the Hydroinformatics Lab, advised by Prof. Ibrahim Demir. My work is on flood impact to critical infrastructure in Louisiana, and more broadly on geospatial AI for natural hazards: susceptibility and impact models that report calibrated uncertainty, physics-informed formulations that carry known process constraints into the network, and explanations that hold up under spatial validation.

Before Tulane I completed a B.Sc. in Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET) in Bangladesh, where my thesis addressed landslide susceptibility mapping under sparse inventory conditions in the Chittagong Hill Tracts. That problem — sparse, spatially clustered labels and models that report more confidence than they have earned — is what moved me from conventional geotechnical analysis toward Bayesian and physics-informed deep learning. I have since worked on landslide and liquefaction assessment for the Chittagong Metropolitan Area as a research engineer, and interned with the Bangladesh Water Development Board.

Research interests

  • Flood susceptibility and impact modeling for critical infrastructure
  • Uncertainty quantification and approximate Bayesian deep learning for geohazards
  • Physics-informed neural networks for geotechnical and hydrological inverse problems
  • Explainable AI and spatially honest validation for geospatial prediction
  • Landslide susceptibility mapping under sparse and spatially clustered inventories
  • Remote sensing and Google Earth Engine workflows for hazard datasets
